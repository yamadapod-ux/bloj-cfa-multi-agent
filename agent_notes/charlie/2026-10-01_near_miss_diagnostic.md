## Executive Summary

**Headline answer: both are true, and they are not in conflict — the market is genuinely expensive on a 25-year-lookback basis, but the Scout Filter pipeline is the more proximate, controllable explanation for 0/155, because a cluster of 2026-09 gate changes (B-V1/D-V1 OR-logic, Filter D naive-FV-only proxy, Street-PT override at exactly 20-25%) is now producing single-digit-percent misses on names with otherwise clean fundamentals, more often than it is cleanly rejecting genuinely overvalued businesses.** Of the ~20 candidates that reached Filter B or deeper since the VALUE-FIRST redesign (2026-09-01) and the D/E trial (2026-09-06), at least 6 died within 5 percentage points (or ≤5% relative) of their threshold — LUV missed Filter D's Street-PT override by 0.66pp (3.3% relative), LMT by ~2.17pp (8-9% relative), VST missed Filter B's FCF-yield bar by 1.2pp (20% relative but an absolute hair), TXT by 0.25-0.3pp, CEG's B-V1 sector-average leg by 1.9% relative, and LRCX's B-V1 peer-median leg by 0.8% relative — the single tightest miss logged in the project. These are not isolated flukes: they recur across both the Value lane (Filter D) and the new Growth lane (B-V1/D-V1), across 5 different sectors, within one month of the newest rule triplet going live.

At the same time, the market-level evidence is real and should not be dismissed: the S&P 500 forward P/E sits at ~19.0-19.5x (three independent sources, cross-checked 2026-09-18 to 2026-09-30), which is close to its 25-year average (16.75) but roughly in the +1 standard-deviation band (band: 13.45-20.04) — a genuinely rich-but-not-bubble reading — while the 10-year Treasury yield has been pinned near 19-year highs (~4.6-4.8%, WSJ confirms a fresh 19-year high as recently as 2026-09-29) with the Fed still hiking (FOMC hiked 2026-09-16 to 3.75-4.00%, another hike priced at 67.5% probability for the 2026-10-28 meeting per CME FedWatch). High discount rates mechanically shrink naive-FV and DCF outputs across every Value-bucket candidate at the exact moment Filter D is asking for a ≥20% valuation gap — so part of what looks like "gate miscalibration" is actually the gate correctly transmitting a genuinely higher cost of capital. The two explanations are complementary, not competing: a tight gate interacting with a genuinely higher-rate, moderately-rich market produces exactly the pattern observed — an increasing count of single-digit-percent misses rather than either (a) comfortable fast-tracks or (b) decisive 20%+ rejects. Recommendations below are presented as options for the CIO to weigh; no filter has been changed.

That safeguard is doing exactly what it was built to do (prevent cherry-picking a single favorable method) — but it means B-V1's "near-miss" count is partly an artifact of a deliberate conservatism rule, not of the underlying threshold being wrong.

**Duration:** the Filter D/E trial was explicitly scoped in CLAUDE.md as "Trial 3 rounds (Round 28-30, post-FOMC → สิ้นปี), review 2026-12-31." The trial has since run far past its originally-scoped 3 rounds — **Round 28 through Round 52 is 25 rounds**, roughly **3.5 weeks of calendar time** (2026-09-06 to 2026-09-30) — with 0 fast-tracks across every single round. In quarter-equivalent terms: the project is ~4.7 months old overall (inception 2026-05-09), i.e. roughly **1.5 quarters total**, and the current D/E rule-set has been live for about **0.4 of a quarter**. Given 25 consecutive rounds (not quarters, but a comparable "a lot of independent draws") at 0%, this comfortably clears any reasonable interpretation of the diagnostic trigger's intent even though it predates the formal 2026-12-31 review date by 3 months.

### (d) Three required diagnostic sub-questions

**(i) Is the market expensive?** — S&P 500 forward P/E, cross-checked ≥2 sources:

- stockmarketperatio.com: forward P/E 19.50x as of 2026-08-31, flagged "Overvalued" on trailing P/E (26.32x) but forward-looking multiple implies the market is pricing ~35% forward earnings growth
- GuruFocus (forward-estimate blended series): 26.18, "2.5% above its long-term average of 25.54" (different methodology/base, but directionally consistent — modestly above its own historical average, not a bubble-level reading)

**Verdict: genuinely moderately rich, not historically extreme.** Multiple independent sources converge on "near +1 standard deviation above the 25-year average, but within 5-year-percentile norms" — this is a real headwind for Filter D's margin-of-safety requirement (a market pricing ~35% forward EPS growth structurally shrinks the gap between price and any *conservative* no-growth-perpetuity FV), but it does not, by itself, explain a literal 0% fast-track rate across 155 tickers — markets have cleared 19-20x forward multiples before with individual sectors still offering genuine 20%+ discounts (which is exactly what the near-miss table shows: dislocated sectors like airlines, aerospace-defense, and utilities/IPP *did* produce real pullbacks, they just fell 1-3 percentage points short of the specific thresholds).

Separately and materially: the **10-year Treasury yield has been pinned at/near 19-year highs** throughout the period in question (WSJ confirms a fresh 19-year high as recently as 2026-09-29; multiple sources show the 10Y oscillating 4.6-4.8% through August-September 2026) with the Fed actively hiking (2026-09-16 FOMC hike to 3.75-4.00%, another hike priced 67.5% for 2026-10-28 per CME FedWatch). This directly and mechanically depresses every Filter D naive-FV calculation (FCF ÷ WACC, where WACC's risk-free-rate input is near a two-decade high) — this is arguably a bigger and more direct driver of Filter D's tight pass rate than the equity multiple itself, because it moves the denominator of literally every naive-FV calculation in the Value bucket simultaneously.

**(iii) Are the gates (D/E specifically) too tight?** — hairline-miss analysis (from 2(b) above) plus learning-queue realized-outcome check:

From (b): **Filter D shows 56% of its deaths within a hairline-or-in-band range** (5 of 9) — this is the single strongest piece of evidence that D specifically (not B, not C) may be calibrated a notch too tight, concentrated in two places: (1) the Street-PT override threshold (20% for D-Legacy, 25% for the tie-break rule) is being missed by low single-digit percentage points repeatedly (LUV 0.66pp, LMT ~2.17pp, PEP ~4.2-4.4pp, AXP ~3.3-4.1pp) rather than being missed decisively; and (2) the fast-track-vs-learning-queue boundary itself (0.80× vs the observed 0.84-0.914× cluster) is catching several candidates (BKNG, KMB, PYPL) within a few percentage points of the easier bar.

**Learning-queue realized-outcome check (6-month price-history test):** this diagnostic sub-question explicitly asks whether any of the project's earliest learning-queue names (naming TMUS, BBWI as examples) would, with hindsight, have been good trades. **The honest finding is that this test cannot yet be run meaningfully — the project is only ~5 months old (inception 2026-05-09) and the learning-queue lane itself only began operating 2026-09-07 (LKQ, the first learning-queue case).** TMUS was flagged 2026-09-26 (Round 34) at a price in the $162-166 range; as of 2026-09-30 TMUS trades at $163.08 — essentially flat, 4 days later, nowhere near a 6-month checkpoint. Any conclusion on gate tightness today rests on the *distribution of miss-distances* (hairline-heavy at D, decisive-heavy at B) rather than on confirmed false-negatives, and should be labeled as such.

## Part 3 — Recommendations for CIO (options only — not implemented)

**Option A — No change, hold the course to the scheduled 2026-12-31 trial review.** The pre-commitment rules (CLAUDE.md § Pre-commitment Rules) explicitly bar changing Return-side-adjacent rules before the rolling-8Q alpha trigger confirms, and Filter D/E were deliberately scoped as a bounded trial with its own 2026-12-31 review date already on the calendar. The hairline-miss cluster at Filter D (56% of D-deaths) is real but is counterbalanced by (a) a genuinely rich-and-rate-elevated macro backdrop that mechanically explains much of why D specifically is binding, and (b) a complete absence of any confirmed false-negative (no learning-queue name has reached its realized-outcome checkpoint yet).
