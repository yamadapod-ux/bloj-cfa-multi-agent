## Part 2 — CIO Hypothesis Verdicts (confirm / refute, with numbers)

### Hypothesis 1: Bear's 30% weight systematically over-penalizes heavy-capex/reinvestment-phase companies by discounting ROIC without distinguishing temporary investment dips from structural impairment

**Verdict: PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, with an important scope correction.**

- The AMZN informal review ($159.72 Bear vs $285.48 Emma-bull / $299.04 margin-adj-comps) is real and is the single most dramatic divergence in the project's history — but it compares Bear's **base/point estimate** against Emma's **bull case and a margin-adjusted (not base) comps figure**, not against Emma's own base case, which was not disclosed in the informal review. This is not yet an apples-to-apples test of whether Bear's 30% weight would flip a *blended* verdict; it needs a formal pipeline run to resolve.
- The **mechanism itself is real and documented elsewhere**: AMZN's own Atlas data package (2026-10-06) explicitly flags a 2-year capex-to-revenue J-curve ("data centers require ~2 years of capital before revenue generation, but can then be monetized for more than 30 years" — management's own language) that a standard ROIC-discounting DCF cannot distinguish from permanent capital destruction without an explicit multi-year reinvestment-phase adjustment. **No such adjustment currently exists in CLAUDE.md's Bear methodology** — this is a genuine, named, structural gap, not merely a complaint.
- **However, this gap is NOT confirmed at scale across the 10 named wide-moat rejects** — as shown in Part 1(c), 8-9 of those 10 never reached a stage where Bear's 30% weight was even computed (they died at scout-stage Filter B/D, run by Charlie/Max before Emma/Quinn/Bear engage at all). **Bear's weighting cannot be the systemic cause of a funnel failure that is happening one or two pipeline stages before Bear is ever invoked.**

### Hypothesis 2: naive-FV-governs-tie-break has failed to find value in wide-moat/capital-intensive businesses ≥8 times, suggesting a structural blind spot

**Verdict: REFUTED AS STATED (the "≥8 times" count), CONFIRMED AS A NARROWER, REAL PATTERN (2-3 cases).**

- Project files directly confirm **VMC** (naive FV $83.5 vs price $243.06, Street PT +32-48%, explicit tie-break rejection) and the **CMCSA Round-46 origin case** (mechanism named after it, but the Round-46 note's own naive-FV-vs-Street-PT numbers were not located in the files reviewed for this diagnostic — recommend direct retrieval before quoting specifics).
- **CCI** is a plausible 3rd case but is explicitly logged as a "methodology-conflict" (ROIC-definition-dependent, MGM-precedent pattern) rather than a clean naive-FV-overrides-Street-PT case — mixing mechanisms would overstate the pattern.
- **LMT, YUM, MCD, DPZ** are independently, specifically documented in project files as dying by **different, already-diagnosed mechanisms** (LMT: Street-PT-override threshold itself narrowly failing at 22.83-23% vs 25%, not naive-FV overriding a passing PT; YUM/MCD/DPZ: decisive Filter B multiple/FCF-yield fails). **UPS and BWXT/POWL could not be located as dated, detailed full-pipeline or scout entries with the specific naive-FV-tie-break mechanism in the watchlist/buy_list/decisions files reviewed.**
- **This does not mean the underlying concern is wrong** — the 2026-10-01 diagnostic's independent finding that **56% of Filter-D deaths are hairline-or-in-band** (not decisive) is a real, large-sample confirmation that Filter D specifically is binding tightly on wide-moat/capital-intensive names generally, even if the CIO's specific "8 named tickers via this specific mechanism" count is not fully supported by the files. **Recommend re-stating the finding as: "Filter D is calibrated too tight for wide-moat names at current (19-year-high) rates, evidenced by a 56% hairline-miss rate across 9 Filter-D deaths" rather than "the naive-FV-tie-break rule has failed 8 named wide-moat names."**

### Hypothesis 3: the 3% Terminal Growth Rate ceiling may be too conservative for genuine secular-growth/wide-moat names

**Verdict: CONFIRMED AS A REAL, DOCUMENTED, RECURRING FRICTION — but with evidence it is a sanity-ceiling, not an auto-reject, and the project's own files show it catching genuine errors as often as it may be under-crediting durable growth.**

- The TGR ceiling has concretely and measurably moved verdicts: **ADSK's 2026-09-12 TGR Compliance Fix** (4.0%→3.0%) alone cut Blended FV ~5.9% and compressed MOS from +11.35% to +4.42% — the **thinnest MOS buffer of any open position**, purely from a compliance correction, not new negative information.
- **NOW's 2026-08-24** TGR correction (stacked with WACC/margin fixes) compressed MOS from +26.6% to +4.42%(ADSK)/flipped NOW's own MOS negative — both are documented, real, repeatable frictions from the 3% ceiling interacting with genuinely durable-growth SaaS names.
- **However**, the TGR ceiling also correctly caught violations that would have overstated FV: TDG's Reverse-DCF implied perpetual growth of 6.27% (nearly 2x the ceiling) was a genuine methodology red flag, not an unfair penalty — the ceiling is doing its sanity-check job in that direction too.
- **Net verdict: the 3% TGR ceiling is a real, quantifiable drag on wide-moat/secular-growth names' FV (and hence MOS and deploy-gate clearance) specifically because the project's DCF terminal value is typically 65-75%+ of total EV for these names (ADSK, ETN, TDG all disclosed this)** — a small TGR change has an outsized effect on exactly the companies with the longest competitive runways. This is a legitimate, data-backed candidate for review, but it is a **Return-side-adjacent methodology parameter embedded inside the Blended FV calculation**, and CLAUDE.md's lock table does not list "TGR ceiling" by name — it is more defensible to treat it as a DCF-input convention (changeable via Charlie/Emma methodology note, same tier as the DCF Cash Flow Consistency Rule) rather than a hard Return-side lock, but this is an interpretive judgment call, not an explicit CLAUDE.md carve-out (see Part 4).

### Hypothesis 4: 3-5yr stated horizon vs CIO's actual 10yr+ preference creates a WACC/TGR/DCF horizon mismatch

**Verdict: CONFIRMED — this is a direct, textual contradiction, not an inference.**

- CLAUDE.md line 18 states explicitly: **"Time horizon: 3–5 ปี"** (3-5 years) under Investment Philosophy — this is the IPS's own stated horizon, unchanged since inception (approved 2026-05-15).
- The CIO's stated preference this session (10-year+ holding horizon) is **structurally inconsistent** with (a) a 3% TGR ceiling calibrated as a "sanity ceiling" against near-term GDP-growth-rate assumptions rather than a multi-decade compounding assumption, (b) WACC inputs built on current-cycle CAPM (beta + current Rf + ERP) rather than a through-cycle or long-run equilibrium rate, and (c) the Re-Analysis Trigger Rule's 90-day staleness + event-driven re-verify cadence, which is tuned for a 3-5yr holding pattern's natural earnings-cycle rhythm, not a 10yr+ buy-and-hold pattern that would tolerate (or even expect) longer stretches without re-verification.
- **This is a genuine, confirmed, foundational-documents-level inconsistency that predates and is independent of the funnel-conversion-rate question.** It cannot be resolved by this diagnostic (which is scoped to the funnel trigger) — it requires either (a) a formal IPS amendment changing the stated horizon to match the CIO's actual preference, with cascading updates to WACC/TGR methodology, or (b) the CIO explicitly confirming 3-5yr remains the fund's actual operating horizon and the 10yr+ comment was a stated personal preference not yet formalized into the IPS. **Recommend Charlie escalate this specific item to the CIO as a standalone IPS-clarification question, separate from the funnel diagnostic's rule-change recommendations below**, because any TGR/WACC change motivated by hypothesis 3 above should be sized consistently with whichever horizon the CIO actually confirms — not addressed in isolation.
