## 🔄 Sector Rotation — US Adtech / Martech Cycle

**กำลัง outperform:** Energy (oil premium), defensive value
**กำลัง underperform:** high-multiple software / growth (Fed hawkish repricing), consumer discretionary

### Adtech / Martech sector view (feeds Sector Views entry — new sector, no prior entry)

**1. Digital ad spend — decelerating but still growing.** eMarketer: US digital ad spend +7.9% in 2026 (programmatic display +12.5%, social +15.6%), but growth rates are "normalizing as digital maturity sets in" — another leg down expected 2027+. Meta family of apps overtakes Google on a net US ad-revenue basis for the first time (~$100.9B). Growth is concentrating in social + retail media; the independent adtech middle is being squeezed.

**2. Retail media networks = the competitive gravity well.** Global retail media market >$196B in 2026; RMN spend ~$69B and now "foundational to how retailers drive profitability," not incremental. Amazon, Walmart Connect, and retailer-owned networks are absorbing budget that used to flow through DSPs and independent martech. This is a structural headwind for anyone not sitting on first-party retail/commerce data.

**3. The Trade Desk (the bellwether) is struggling.** TTD Q2 revenue guide (~$750M) read "soft," stock fell hard after the May call; "conflicts over margin control ... indicative of a shrinking addressable market" (Digiday). TTD TTM revenue growth ~11.6% (per data package) vs ZETA's 35% total / 25% organic — the pure-play DSP model is maturing fast while ZETA's identity-graph + owned-channel model is still compounding.

**4. Enterprise martech budgets — constrained, but AI is the priority.** Gartner 2026 CMO Spend: overall marketing spend +1.7% YoY (weakest since 2021); martech share of marketing budget at a 5-year low (19.4%, down from 26.6% in 2021) — BUT 62% of CMOs plan to invest MORE in martech, 15.3% of budgets now to AI, 70% name AI their top H2 2026 priority, and 56% are shifting martech spend to consumption/usage-based pricing. Read: budget is scarce and consolidating toward fewer, AI-differentiated, outcome-priced platforms. ZETA's "software + media/data pass-through" consumption model fits the pricing shift; its AI/agentic narrative fits the priority — but only if it can prove ROI vs point tools.

**5. AI-in-marketing / "agentic AI."** 90.3% of surveyed marketing teams now run AI agents somewhere in the stack; but 74% of martech leaders cite data integration/quality as the top barrier to agentic adoption. This is exactly the wedge ZETA is pitching (proprietary Identity Graph as the data layer + OpenAI / Snowflake / Palantir partnerships). Narrative tailwind, execution-dependent, and crowded — every martech vendor is making the same claim.

**6. Martech landscape consolidation.** 2026 martech landscape: 15,505 products, +0.79% — flattest growth in 15 years; 1,488 added / 1,367 removed. Heavy churn = consolidation phase; scaled platforms with data moats gain share from sub-scale point tools, but the incremental TAM is not expanding.

**Net sector read: CAUTIOUS.** Secular digital-ad growth is real but decelerating; the budget pool is constrained and consolidating; retail media + Meta/Google are absorbing the growth; the pure-play adtech bellwether (TTD) is de-rating on a shrinking addressable market. Scaled players with genuine first-party data + owned activation channels (ZETA's pitch) can still take share, but (a) the AI-in-marketing narrative is universal and unproven at enterprise scale, (b) Fed-hawkish repricing hits the whole high-multiple cohort, and (c) ZETA carries a stock-specific data-provenance overhang (Culper / class action, below). Selective, not broad-buy.

This is an adverse procedural development for ZETA — the claims survived the pleading stage. Sources: Stanford Securities Class Action Clearinghouse (case id 108483); Labaton Keller Sucharow; Kessler Topaz; ppc.land ("Judge forces Zeta Global to face suit over 240 million opt-in claim").
