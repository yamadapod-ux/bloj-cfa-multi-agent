### 🏦 Fed Policy — what it means for a deposit-funded consumer lender [CFA L1: Economics — Monetary Policy]
- SLM's assets are mostly **variable-rate private education loans**, and its liabilities are deposits plus ABS. In Q2-26, NIM was **4.75%**, down 56 bps YoY. Part of that came from excess liquidity after the March loan sale. ([2Q26 8-K Ex.99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001032033/000103203326000041/slm072326ex991.htm))
- A hawkish Fed has two effects: (+) asset yields reprice up; (−) deposit beta and brokerage funding costs rise, and the borrower payment burden goes up, which flows into NCOs. **Rates are a second-order driver. The first-order driver is credit.**
- The 10Y is 5.10%, which is high, and it mechanically raises cost of equity. Every 25 bps of Rf moves CoE by about 25 bps.
