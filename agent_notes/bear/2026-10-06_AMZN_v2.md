## The rule being applied
Every negative factor that discounts ROIC/FCF must be labeled **either**:
1. **"Temporary/reinvestment-phase depression"** — deliberate, disclosed capex/R&D program with a monetization timeline, OR
2. **"Structural/permanent impairment"** — competitive erosion/secular decline/execution failure with no credible recovery path.

The morning's Bear note blended these together into one undifferentiated discount. Below, every negative factor from the morning's 6 Key Challenge Points is re-classified individually, with real data, before re-deriving a downside FV.

## Re-deriving Bear's downside FV with the classification made explicit

**Key change in methodology:** the morning's Bear FV applied a single blended discount (via an undifferentiated weighted scenario mix) to AMZN's ROIC/FCF profile. With the classification now explicit, Bear separates the DCF's bear-case inputs into the piece attributable to temporary reinvestment-phase factors (which SHOULD mean-revert within the explicit 10yr horizon, consistent with Emma's own FCF-margin-recovery path) vs the piece attributable to genuinely structural factors (share erosion + antitrust overhang, which should NOT be assumed to mean-revert and deserves a harder, more persistent haircut).

- **Temporary-phase-driven components (factors 1a [70%], 2):** these are *already* reflected in Emma's base-case DCF via the explicit FCF-margin-recovery path (0%→15.0% over 10 years) — Bear does **not** need to apply an *additional* discount on top of Emma's own capex-normalization assumption for this portion, because double-counting a temporary factor that Emma's base case already prices in would overstate the downside. Bear's bear-case DCF ($59.89, unchanged mechanical calculation: Y1 growth 11%, WACC 13.0%, TGR 2.0%) already stress-tests this by assuming a slower, more stressed recovery path — that is the correct place for temporary-phase risk to live.
- **Structural components (factors 1b [30% of ROIC gap — retail mix drag], 3 [AWS share loss], 4 [antitrust overhang]):** these deserve an *incremental, persistent* haircut beyond Emma's bear-case DCF, because they represent risks that do NOT resolve within the 10-year explicit horizon the way a capex cycle does. Quantifying:
  - AWS share-loss structural discount: if AWS's share continues eroding at the current ~2pp/year pace, terminal-year AWS revenue mix (and therefore consolidated terminal margin, which depends heavily on AWS's ~61%-of-profit contribution) could be **5-8% lower** than Emma's base case assumes by Year 10. Applied to Emma's bear-case DCF ($59.89) as a terminal-value haircut: **-$4.50/share** (≈7.5% of $59.89).
  - Antitrust overhang: unresolved, binary — Bear assigns an explicit probability-weighted haircut rather than leaving it fully unpriced (improvement in rigor vs morning's "unpriced" treatment): ~15% probability of a structurally damaging outcome (advertising-segment remedy/fine materially impairing the segment's economics) × an estimated 20% permanent impairment to Advertising-segment value (Ads ≈$19.8B/qtr, roughly $79B annualized, conservatively ~8-10% of consolidated EV) ≈ **-$2.40/share** expected-value haircut.
  - Retail-mix structural drag (30% of factor 1): already substantially reflected in AMZN's low blended margin assumptions in Emma's own DCF (consolidated, not AWS-only, margins) — **no further incremental haircut applied** to avoid double-counting what Emma's model already structurally bakes in via the blended (not AWS-only) revenue/margin path.

**Bear's revised bear-case DCF FV: $59.89 - $4.50 - $2.40 = $53.00** (vs morning's unadjusted $59.89 — a modest, now-explicitly-justified incremental haircut for the two factors genuinely classified structural, replacing what was previously an undifferentiated blend).
