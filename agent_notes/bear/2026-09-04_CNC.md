[Source: Healthcare Dive "'Back on track': Centene swings to more than $1B in profit in Q2"](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/centene-cnc-q2-2026-guidance-hike-aca-medicaid/826286/); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/centene-boosts-2026-guidance-it-posts-11b-q2-profit)

- **Quinn** stripped an estimated **$0.27/share** after-tax, arriving at ex-one-time EPS **$4.53**.
- **Actual disclosed one-time benefit is ~$0.50/share** (analyst-cited figure, covering Marketplace risk-adjustment + Medicare settlements together) — **both analysts under-corrected by $0.06–$0.14/share.**
- **Analysts on the earnings call themselves pressed management specifically on "the timing of margin recovery" and "reliance on settlement proceeds"** — i.e., the sell-side is already flagging this as a live quality-of-earnings question, not a resolved non-issue.

**Verdict: Emma's "normalized forward P/E ~15.3x, in line with sector" claim is directionally right but the multiple is understated** — true normalized EPS is closer to $4.30 than $4.44, so true normalized forward P/E ≈ **15.8x**, *above* the 16.59x sector median only by a hair less, i.e., CNC is now **essentially AT or slightly above** the sector median on a fully-corrected basis — the "cheapest in peer group" framing is even weaker than Emma already concluded it was.

### 2. ACA risk-adjustment miss — is it fixed or is it a spring being re-loaded?

**Finding:** Centene has **"commenced refiling 2026 Marketplace rates to reflect higher projected baseline morbidity and [is taking] corrective pricing actions for 2026 in states representing a substantial majority of its Marketplace membership."** [Source: SEC 8-K, via WebSearch summary of CMS/company disclosures]

This is a genuine structural fix at the pricing level — not merely a one-time accounting catch-up. **But it is a double-edged fix.** Raising Marketplace premiums to correct for morbidity mis-pricing, layered on top of the **enhanced PTC subsidy cliff** (avg. subsidized-enrollee premium already more than doubled from $888→$1,904/year per KFF, per Atlas §H4), is **the exact same adverse-selection mechanism that caused the 2025 crisis in the first place**: healthier/price-sensitive enrollees drop out first when premiums rise, leaving a sicker residual risk pool, which raises morbidity further, which could force another repricing/reserve miss cycle. **This is not resolved risk — it is the same feedback loop, now compounding on itself for a second consecutive year with less subsidy cushion than 2025 had.**

- **Governance angle:** the root cause of 2025 wasn't "growth was lower than expected" in isolation — it was that Centene's actuarial team **mis-modeled the risk pool ahead of open enrollment while regulatory/subsidy conditions were changing underneath them.** The same regulatory conditions (PTC cliff) are still in flux for the 2026-2027 open enrollment cycles. **The probability of a repeat miss is not zero and arguably not small** — this supports Quinn's Scenario 4 "Severe" (15% probability, FV $17.50) being kept, not shaved down.

### 3. ROIC < WACC even normalized — confirmed by both analysts independently, Bear agrees this is real

Emma: normalized ROIC 4.0–5.7% vs WACC 8.14%. Quinn: forward ROIC 7.83% (no-cash-net) vs WACC 8.32% — "marginal/borderline." **Bear's read: use the no-cash-netting convention as primary** (both analysts and Atlas agree this is the theoretically correct convention for an insurer, since most of the $24B cash backs statutory reserves, not deployable capital) — on that basis, **ROIC sits below WACC in every scenario Emma ran, and marginally below WACC even in Quinn's most favorable non-cash-netted forward case.** This is **not a TTM artifact that will "fix itself"** — it's a structurally thin-margin, government-payer-dependent, buyer-power-constrained business (Emma's own Porter's Five Forces: Buyer Power = High). **A company that cannot durably earn above its cost of capital does not deserve a "quality" P/E — the multiple compression risk here is real, not manufactured bear pessimism.**

### 4. ACA Marketplace exposure — quantified for the first time this session (Emma/Atlas flagged as N/A)

**New finding:** Centene's **Commercial segment (predominantly Marketplace) = ~21.6% of total revenue**; Medicaid = ~75.8%; Other = ~2.6%. [Source: TickerGate revenue breakdown via WebSearch, cross-referenced against Becker's Payer Issues Marketplace membership figures]

This directly answers the CIO's question: **CNC's ACA exposure is materially smaller than OSCR's 100%, but it is not trivial — roughly 1 in every 5 revenue dollars flows through the exact segment that caused the entire company to swing from >$7.25 guided adjusted EPS to a **-$13.53 GAAP loss** in FY2025.** That is a striking asymmetry: a 21.6%-of-revenue segment single-handedly took down 100% of consolidated earnings for a full year. **This is the key reason CNC's downside floor is NOT as protected as "diversified, 3-segment business" framing (Quinn's conviction rationale) implies** — Medicaid being 75.8% of revenue doesn't insulate the P&L from a Marketplace-segment blowup, because that's exactly what happened in 2025 with a *smaller* Marketplace book (5.9M members then vs 3.5M now — the book has actually shrunk 41% YoY, so the segment's *revenue share* concentration risk may be moderating, but the risk-pool morbidity mechanics are unchanged or worse).

**Comparison to OSCR:** OSCR = 100% ACA-exposed, binary legislative bet. CNC = ~22% ACA-exposed by revenue, diversified by Medicaid, **but with a demonstrated (2025) history of that 22% single-handedly wiping out full-year profitability.** Bear's view: **this is a meaningfully better risk profile than OSCR (agrees with Quinn), but "diversified enough to ignore the segment" is the wrong conclusion — it should instead be modeled as "22% exposure with a proven ability to cause 100%+ consolidated EPS swings," which is closer to what Quinn's P-W EV scenarios already do (correctly) than what the qualitative "diversified" framing implies on its own.**

### 5. Medicaid 6-month redetermination — status confirmed as forward, unpriced headwind (Atlas §H3 verified, no new contradicting data found)

No new information found this session that changes Atlas's assessment: rollout begins Dec 31, 2026 (Nebraska already live since May 2026), full compliance by Jan 1, 2027. **This is a genuine secular headwind that starts right as the market is being asked to underwrite CNC's "recovery is durable" thesis** — the redetermination acceleration and the ACA re-pricing risk (#2 above) land in the same 12-18 month window. **Compounding-risk timing, not independent risks that diversify each other.**

### 6. Governance red flag — confirmed still open, not fully resolved

**Finding:** Most recent PBM-overbilling settlement found is **New Hampshire, $21.1M, dated as recently as September 2025** — less than 12 months old at time of this analysis. **At least 20 states have settled since 2021; Georgia and Florida remain holdouts (unresolved litigation).** [Source: Healthcare Dive NH settlement](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/centene-reaches-settlement-new-hampshire-pbm/616894/); [KFF Health News — "Years Later, Centene Settlements With States Still Unfinished"](https://kffhealthnews.org/courts/centene-settlements-pbms-medicaid-silence-holdouts-georgia-florida/)

Company has also announced it will **outsource its ~$35B/year PBM spend to a third-party via RFP** — an implicit admission the in-house PBM (Centene's own Envolve, the entity at the center of the overbilling scandal) could not be trusted/fixed internally. This is a *positive* governance remediation step, but it also confirms the underlying problem was structural, not a one-off error, and the transition itself carries execution risk (new vendor, new contract terms, integration).

**Bear's challenge to Emma's ESG scorecard:** Emma's G score of **4/10** is reasonable but arguably still 1 point too generous. Given (a) most recent settlement is <12 months old, (b) 2 states remain in active unresolved litigation, (c) the root failure mode (actuarial/risk-pool mis-modeling) is the *same* failure mode now sitting live in the 2026-2027 ACA repricing cycle (#2 above) — **Bear recommends G = 3/10**, moving Overall ESG from 5.7/10 to **5.3/10**. This is a minor scorecard adjustment (not a valuation-moving item on its own — already substantially captured in Emma's -2-3% governance valuation haircut) but the CIO specifically asked Bear to challenge the ESG number, and Bear's view is it should skew slightly more cautious, not less.

## What Would Change Our Mind

**Bull Flip Triggers (3-5, measurable):**
1. Consolidated HBR sustains ≤90% for **4 consecutive quarters** (Q3 2026 – Q2 2027) — proves structural, not settlement-driven, margin recovery
2. FY2027 initial guidance (expected ~Jan/Feb 2027) shows normalized (ex-one-time) adjusted EPS ≥$4.80 on a **clean basis**, with company explicitly disclosing zero one-time items embedded — removes the quality-of-earnings overhang from Challenge #1
3. Congress extends enhanced ACA PTC subsidies in any form (CARE Act-style or similar) before the next open-enrollment cycle — removes the adverse-selection driver behind both the 2025 crisis and the ongoing repricing risk (Challenge #2)
4. Price falls to ≤$47 (Blended FV zone, using Emma/Quinn's central estimates) — MOS ≥15% Value-bucket gate satisfied
5. Medicaid membership attrition through the Dec-2026-onward 6-month redetermination rollout tracks **below** current run-rate expectations (i.e., churn less severe than the sector fears)

**Bear Flip Triggers (3-5, measurable — downgrade to stronger AVOID/SELL if held):**
1. Any new actuarial/risk-adjustment guidance miss disclosed in FY2026 or FY2027 (a repeat of the 2025 pattern) — confirms Challenge #2's "spring reloading" thesis
2. Consolidated HBR reverses upward (e.g., back above 92%) in any single quarter
3. Marketplace membership decline accelerates materially beyond the current pace (5.9M→3.5M YoY, already -41%) — signals an adverse-selection spiral is underway, not stabilizing
4. A new PBM-related settlement or judgment exceeding $100M in a single state (GA/FL holdout resolution or new claim) — signals the governance issue (Challenge #6) is not closed
5. Insider selling by the CFO/General Counsel (Christopher Koster sold ~104K shares in Aug 2026 near $65-67 — noted, not yet alarming) accelerates into a broader pattern across multiple Section 16 officers

**Thesis Invalidation (re-analysis required):**
- FY2026 full-year results show ex-one-time recurring EPS meaningfully **below** the Bear-corrected $4.11–$4.30 estimate (i.e., a fresh actuarial miss, not just "recovery was overstated by one accounting quarter") — this would confirm the ACA risk-adjustment problem is a recurring structural feature of CNC's business model, not a 2025 one-off, and would justify moving CNC to a "structural value trap" characterization rather than "post-crisis recovery re-rating too far, too fast."
- **Conversely:** 2+ consecutive quarters where HBR holds ≤90% AND adjusted EPS beats the $4.80 guidance on a fully clean (zero one-time items disclosed) basis would invalidate the Challenge #1 quality-of-earnings concern specifically — though this alone would not flip the valuation call unless price also falls or FV rises enough to close the MOS gap.
