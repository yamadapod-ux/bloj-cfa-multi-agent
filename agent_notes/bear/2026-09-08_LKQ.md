### 4.2 ROIC ≈ WACC → no economic moat

- **LKQ invested capital** = Total Debt $5,313M + Book Equity $6,471M − Cash $301M = **$11,483M — solidly positive.** So the "negative-equity ROIC distortion check" (learning-log; FICO/VEEV/TDG) **does NOT apply here** — ROIC 6.35% is a **real, meaningful number**, not an artifact.
- **Emma's rebuttal** ("normalized ROIC ~7.8% on total invested capital; ~11–13% ex-goodwill") is a **methodological dodge**:
  - You cannot exclude $5.4B of goodwill that was paid in **real cash and stock** and then claim high returns. The capital *was* deployed. ROIC on **total invested capital** is what determines value creation for the shareholder who funded those acquisitions.
  - "ROIC ex-goodwill = 11–13%" answers the question "would this be a good business if we hadn't overpaid?" — which is **not the investment question**.
- **Even taking Emma's own normalized ROIC of 7.8%** vs **Bear WACC 8.5–8.75%** (§6) → **ROIC < WACC even normalized** = value destruction at the margin. Vs Emma's own WACC 8.0% → ROIC ≈ WACC = a coin flip.
- **CFA L2 definition:** an economic moat requires **sustainable ROIC meaningfully above WACC**. LKQ clears this only at the FY2022–23 through-cycle peak (10–11%) — the very period Emma's Bridge A says was inflated by COVID scrap/PGM prices that "never return."

### 4.3 MOAT VERDICT

**NONE to VERY-NARROW.** Emma's "Narrow" is **one notch too generous.**
- The salvage/recycling vertical integration is a **real cost advantage** — but it is (a) **narrowing** (AI auction pricing, −200–300bps salvage GM), (b) deployed in a **structurally shrinking** addressable market (repairable claims −9.7%, sub-$2k appraisals 41.5%→25.5%, ADAS 28.3%+), and (c) confers **share defense, not pricing power** (Emma concedes this).
- A cost advantage in a shrinking market = **managing decline efficiently**, which is what "best of a bad group" means. It is not a moat that compounds intrinsic value.
- Durability of even the cost advantage: **< 10 years**.
