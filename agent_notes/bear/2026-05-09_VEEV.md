## Challenge #3: ROIC 115.9% = Accounting Artifact? [CFA L2: DuPont / ROIC Analysis]

### Emma's Claim
ROIC 115.9% = extraordinary evidence of wide moat and capital efficiency.

### Bear's Counter
SaaS companies have structurally inflated ROIC because R&D is expensed (not capitalized) under GAAP:

**GAAP ROIC Calculation Problem:**
- Invested Capital (IC) = Equity + Debt - Cash = understated because R&D not on balance sheet
- VEEV spends ~$500-600M/yr on R&D (expensed) → not in IC denominator
- If we capitalize 5 years of R&D ($2.5-3B cumulative) and amortize over 5 years:
  - Adjusted IC increases by $1.5-2B
  - Adjusted ROIC = NOPAT / (Original IC + $1.5B) → drops significantly

**Bear's Estimate:**
- Original GAAP IC: ~$800M (asset-light)
- R&D-Capitalized Addition: +$2.0B (5-year cumulative, 5-year straight-line amortize)
- Adjusted IC: ~$2.8B
- NOPAT: ~$900M (Op income adjusted)
- **Adjusted ROIC: ~32%** (still very good, but NOT 115.9%)

**Conclusion:** 32% adjusted ROIC is still top-quartile and confirms moat, but Emma's "ROIC 115.9% = top 1% of all software" is a GAAP artifact. The narrative overstates the degree of moat superiority.
