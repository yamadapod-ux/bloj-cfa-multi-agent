## 1. Business Overview [CFA L2: Business Analysis — Competitive Positioning]

### How Does Intuit Make Money? (Business Deep Dive)

Intuit operates a **multi-sided financial platform** with three distinct monetization engines:

```
INTUIT REVENUE ENGINE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Segment 1: SMALL BUSINESS & SELF-EMPLOYED (~55% Revenue)
  └── QuickBooks Online (QBO): ~$450/yr ARPU, 10.1M subscribers
  └── QuickBooks Enterprise: ~$3,000–$8,000/yr, ~400K users
  └── QuickBooks Payroll: ~$75–$125/employee/mo; 1.9M employers
  └── QuickBooks Payments: Transaction fees 2.4–3.5%; $100B+ volume
  └── Mailchimp: $20–$350/mo; marketing automation for SMBs
  └── Intuit Enterprise Suite: $3,000+/yr; mid-market (newest, high ASP)

Segment 2: CONSUMER (~29% Revenue)
  └── TurboTax Free Edition: $0 (freemium, basic returns)
  └── TurboTax Deluxe/Premium: $59–$119 (investments, rental income)
  └── TurboTax Self-Employed: $119–$169 (Schedule C filers)
  └── TurboTax Live: $169–$419 (human expert assistance)
  └── TurboTax Full Service: $199–$599 (done-for-you)

Segment 3: CREDIT KARMA (~11% Revenue)
  └── Credit Card Recommendations: Lead gen fees ($50–$200 per approved card)
  └── Personal Loans: Lead gen fees
  └── Home Loans/Mortgage: Lead gen fees (highest per-lead value ~$500+)
  └── Auto Insurance: Lead gen + comparison fees
  └── Credit Karma Money (Savings): Interchange + float income

Segment 4: PROTAX (~5% Revenue)
  └── Lacerte (CPA software): Enterprise tax prep for professionals
  └── ProConnect: Cloud-based CPA platform
  └── ProSeries: Small CPA firm solution
```

**Revenue Quality Assessment:**
- 70%+ of revenue is recurring/subscription (SaaS-like)
- TurboTax = seasonal (Q3 FY is Jan–Apr; contributes 40% of annual Consumer rev)
- QuickBooks = pure recurring SaaS
- Credit Karma = variable/transactional (advertising-like model)

## 2. Porter's Five Forces Analysis [CFA L2: Industry Analysis — Porter's Framework]

### Force 1: Rivalry Among Existing Competitors — MEDIUM
- TurboTax vs H&R Block Digital: Duopoly in consumer tax — INTU leads with ~30% unit share
- QuickBooks vs Xero/FreshBooks: INTU dominant in US (70%+ SMB share), Xero dominant in APAC
- Credit Karma vs Bankrate/NerdWallet: INTU leads in member scale (130M vs NerdWallet 38M)
- **Assessment: MEDIUM rivalry — INTU dominates in US but faces accelerating digital competition**

### Force 2: Threat of New Entrants — LOW-MEDIUM
- Tax compliance: IRS/state e-filing API integrations = major barrier (2–3 yr build)
- Accounting software: API ecosystem (banks, payroll, payments) = barrier
- BUT: AI startups can build tax tools with LLMs in 12–18 months
- Capital: Low barrier for VC-funded challengers
- **Assessment: LOW-MEDIUM — high in regulated compliance; medium in AI-enabled new tools**

### Force 3: Threat of Substitutes — MEDIUM-HIGH
- IRS Direct File: FREE government service — expanding rapidly (40+ states 2026)
- Open-source accounting: Wave (now Block), GnuCash — free basic accounting
- AI-powered DIY: ChatGPT/Claude for simple tax scenarios
- Excel/Sheets: Still used by ~25% of small businesses for basic bookkeeping
- **Assessment: MEDIUM-HIGH — IRS Direct File = highest structural substitute threat**

### Force 4: Bargaining Power of Customers — MEDIUM
- TurboTax customers: Low switching cost for free/basic filers; MEDIUM for complex filers
- QuickBooks: Once integrated + using payroll/payments, switching cost HIGH (data migration + workflow disruption)
- Credit Karma: Zero switching cost — members use multiple platforms
- **Assessment: MEDIUM overall — QuickBooks has strong lock-in; TurboTax/CK lower**

### Force 5: Bargaining Power of Suppliers — LOW
- Key inputs: Cloud computing (AWS/Azure/GCP) — competitive market
- No proprietary hardware dependency
- Data (tax forms, IRS APIs): Government-provided, non-exclusive
- AI models: Multiple providers (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google) — INTU not locked into one
- **Assessment: LOW — INTU has scale advantage in cloud procurement**

**Porter's Summary Score: 3.2/5 — Competitive pressure moderate; INTU's moat holds but faces IRS Direct File as structural threat**

## 3. Economic Moat Analysis [CFA L2: Economic Moat — Switching Costs + Network Effects + Intangibles]

### Moat Sources:

**A. Network Effects (STRONG for QuickBooks ecosystem)**
- QuickBooks App Marketplace: 750+ integrated apps (banks, payroll, inventory, POS)
- Each new integration makes QBO more valuable → classic network moat
- QuickBooks Payments tied to QBO → transaction data creates accounting entries automatically
- **Score: 8/10 — strongest moat source**

**B. Switching Costs (STRONG for QuickBooks + TurboTax)**
- QuickBooks switching: Requires migrating 3–7 years of financial records, rebinding 750+ integrations, retraining staff
- Estimated switching cost: 200–400+ hours of work for typical SMB
- TurboTax switching: Prior year returns, saved documents — moderate barrier
- Prior-year return data lock-in: 87% annual TurboTax retention rate (industry leading)
- **Score: 8.5/10 — very high for QBO, moderate for TurboTax**

**C. Intangible Assets (DATA MOAT — strongest in class)**
- 100M+ consumer financial profiles (Credit Karma + TurboTax combined)
- 10M+ SMB financial profiles (QuickBooks)
- These profiles span: income, spending, credit, taxes, payroll, payments
- **Only Intuit has full-picture financial data across personal + business + tax domains**
- IRS accuracy guarantee on TurboTax → brand trust
- **Score: 9/10 — data moat is exceptionally rare and defensible**

**D. Scale Advantages**
- Engineering: $2.8B+ R&D annual spend (highest in financial software category)
- Distribution: 90M+ customer touchpoints annually (47M TurboTax + 10M QB + 130M CK)
- Regulatory expertise: 40 years of tax code integration → institutional knowledge
- **Score: 7.5/10**

**Overall Moat: WIDE** [CFA L2: Wide Moat = sustainable competitive advantage expected 10+ years]
- Moat durability: 8/10
- Moat risk: IRS Direct File (structural), AI disruption (medium-term)
