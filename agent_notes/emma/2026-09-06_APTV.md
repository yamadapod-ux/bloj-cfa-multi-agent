## 5. Business Deep Dive [CFA L2: Industry & Company Analysis]

### 5a. "How does Aptiv make money?" (plain language)

- **Sells the electrical "nervous system" and "brain" of a vehicle to automakers.** Connectors, wiring architecture, power distribution, high-voltage components for EVs (Engineered Components Group), plus ADAS sensors, central compute, and software (Intelligent Systems).
- **Gets designed into a vehicle platform years before production**, then earns revenue on every unit built for that platform's ~5–7 year life. Revenue tracks global vehicle production volumes × Aptiv's dollar content per vehicle.
- **Content per vehicle rises** as cars get more electrified (EVs need far more high-voltage wiring/connectors) and more software-defined (more sensors, more compute). That's the growth engine — independent of unit volume.
- **Increasingly sells outside autos** — connectors and components for industrial machinery, aerospace & defense, robotics/drones (targeting ~$300M, +12% YoY). Diversification away from the auto cycle.
- **Returns almost all free cash flow to shareholders via buybacks** (~100% of FCF in 2026), shrinking the share count ~3%/yr and levering per-share earnings.

### 5b. Porter's Five Forces [CFA L2: Industry Analysis — Five Forces]

| Force | Level | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Supplier Power** | **Medium** | Key inputs: copper, semiconductors, resins/plastics. Commodity cost pass-through to OEMs lags 1–2 quarters. Semiconductor availability improved from 2021–22 crisis but remains a periodic constraint. |
| **Buyer Power** | **HIGH** | OEMs are large, concentrated, sophisticated buyers who demand annual price-downs (~2–3%/yr), can dual-source, and can (and do) in-source — e.g. GM's share of APTV sales fell from ~14% (2022) to <10% (2025). Chinese OEMs squeeze supplier margins hardest amid their price war. |
| **New Entrants** | **Low–Medium** | High barriers: capital, engineering depth, multi-year OEM qualification, safety/functional-safety certification. BUT Chinese local suppliers are entering aggressively and winning share in China as local OEMs favour local supply chains. |
| **Substitutes** | **Low–Medium** | Wiring, connectors, power distribution are physically essential. BUT zonal/domain architecture *reduces* total harness content per vehicle (fewer, shorter runs), and OEM in-house software substitutes for parts of Intelligent Systems. |
| **Rivalry** | **HIGH** | Fragmented, global, and intense: Bosch, Continental, ZF, Valeo, TE Connectivity, Amphenol, Yazaki, Sumitomo, Molex, plus rising Chinese players. Industry overcapacity + flat volumes + tariff cost overhang = price competition. |

**Industry Attractiveness: LOW–MEDIUM.** Structurally tough (buyer power + rivalry), partially offset by the secular content-per-vehicle tailwind and APTV's above-average margin mix.
