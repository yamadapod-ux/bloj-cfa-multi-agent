## 🏢 Business Deep Dive (mandatory, CIO-mandated 2-4 sentence description up front)

**What does Amazon actually do?** Amazon.com operates across three reportable segments. **North America** and **International** retail segments run the e-commerce marketplace (online/physical stores, third-party seller services, subscriptions like Prime, and the fast-growing Advertising business layered on top of retail traffic) — together ~79% of consolidated revenue but structurally lower-margin (7.9% and 4.1% operating margin respectively in Q2 2026). **AWS (Amazon Web Services)** is the world's largest cloud infrastructure provider (28% global share), renting compute, storage, database, and — increasingly — AI/ML infrastructure (training and inference capacity, custom Trainium/Graviton chips, the Bedrock AI platform) to enterprise and startup customers; AWS is structurally the highest-margin segment (39.4% operating margin in Q2 2026) and now generates ~61% of Amazon's total operating profit despite being only ~21% of revenue. The current investment debate centers on whether Amazon's ~$220B 2026 AI-data-center capex program converts AWS's currently-accelerating 36.7% YoY growth into durable, monetizable long-term capacity (management's own framing: ~2-year build lead time, 30-year useful life) or represents overbuild risk in a now-three-way hyperscaler race against Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud.

## How does Amazon make money? (3-5 bullets, plain language)
- **Retail margin on goods sold + commission on third-party sellers** — Amazon takes a cut (commissions, fulfillment fees) on the ~60% of unit sales that come from third-party marketplace sellers, which is a much higher-margin stream than Amazon's own first-party retail.
- **Prime subscription fees** ($13.7B in Q2 2026 alone) — a recurring, high-retention revenue base that also locks in retail/advertising engagement.
- **Advertising** ($19.8B Q2 2026, +26% YoY) — sponsored product placements, display, video; effectively a tax on the retail marketplace's own traffic, very high incremental margin.
- **AWS compute/storage/AI rental** — enterprises pay Amazon to run infrastructure (servers, databases, AI model training/inference) instead of owning their own data centers; highest-margin, fastest-growing segment.
- **Co-branded credit cards, logistics/shipping services sold to third parties, healthcare services** — smaller "Other" revenue lines layered onto the core retail/cloud flywheel.
