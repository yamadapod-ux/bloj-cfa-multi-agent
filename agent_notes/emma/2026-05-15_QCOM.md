## Economic Moat Analysis [CFA L2: Equity — Economic Moat, Porter's 5 Forces]

### Moat Rating: **NARROW+ (approaching Wide)**

**5 Moat Sources:**

1. **Intangible Assets — IP & Patents (STRONG):** QCOM holds ~10B patents in wireless communications (3G/4G/5G). Every smartphone manufacturer globally pays QTL licensing fees. Patent portfolio defensible through 2030s+. QTL generates $4.8B/yr at ~70% margin — pure licensing cash flow with near-zero capex. **Strength: WIDE moat in IP/licensing alone.**

2. **Switching Costs — Chipset Integration (MODERATE):** Snapdragon platform integrates modem + CPU + GPU + AI (NPU) + DSP in a single SoC. OEM engineers design around Snapdragon architecture for 2-3 years. Switching to MediaTek or Samsung requires complete re-engineering cycle. **BUT:** Apple is switching — proves switching IS possible given sufficient motivation and resources. **Strength: NARROW — strong but not unbreakable.**

3. **Scale + R&D Advantage (STRONG):** $7-8B R&D/yr = impossible for most semis to match. Leading-edge process node access via TSMC partnership. Snapdragon 8 Elite leads benchmarks in NPU performance (AI on-device). **Strength: NARROW+ to WIDE — scale creates self-reinforcing R&D advantage.**

4. **Network Effects — QTL Licensing (MODERATE-STRONG):** As more devices use cellular connectivity, QTL licensing base grows. IoT and automotive expanding licensing horizon. **BUT:** FHFA-analog situation — regulators may force licensing fee reductions (FTC Korea fine $865M, EU €997M). **Strength: NARROW — regulatory capped.**

5. **Cost Advantage — Fabless Model (MODERATE):** Fabless semiconductor = no manufacturing capex burden. TSMC manufacturing QCOM designs at best-in-class yield. Capital efficiency superb (CapEx only $1.5B/yr vs $11B FCF). **BUT:** TSMC concentration risk (Taiwan geo-risk). **Strength: NARROW.**

**Overall Moat:** NARROW+ — QTL licensing = Wide moat; QCT chips = Narrow moat; blended = Narrow+ (like MRVL but with stronger IP fortress)

**Moat Sustainability (10-20 years):** MODERATE — IP portfolio defensible; automotive ramp adds duration; Apple modem transition and FHFA-style licensing pressure are the two biggest moat erosion risks.
