## 4. Bear — Devil's Advocate Case

### 1. Semicap-cycle-timing risk — are we near a peak, not a trough?
The bull case treats the 2025-2026 WFE upcycle as early/mid-cycle AI-driven structural demand. The bear case: **capex cycles in semicap equipment have historically been sharply cyclical (2018, 2022-23 downturns both saw 20-30%+ revenue contractions within 12-18 months of a peak)**. Current TTM growth (+26%) is decelerating on a *sequential* quarterly basis in the outer quarters even as YoY comps remain strong (base effects). StockStory (cited in scout) flags analyst projections of system-shipment growth decelerating from 82% (2025) to ~3% (2026) — an extreme deceleration forecast that, if directionally correct, would mean the market is currently pricing LRCX on cycle-peak, not cycle-trough, financials. **A 20-30% off-52W-high price move that is itself a reaction to capex-durability fears (per scout's own framing) can just as easily be the start of a real de-rating as a buying opportunity — the "cyclical/sentiment" classification is a judgment call under genuine uncertainty, not a certainty.**

### 2. Customer concentration risk
LRCX's revenue historically concentrates in a handful of foundry/memory customers (TSMC, Samsung, Micron, SK Hynix class names) — quarterly disclosures have shown 3-4 customers >10% of revenue in various periods. A slowdown or capex pause at any single hyperscaler-adjacent or foundry customer has an outsized P&L impact given this concentration, and memory (NAND/DRAM) capex in particular is historically the most volatile sub-segment of WFE spend.

### 3. China export-control policy risk
China has represented a large, volatile share of LRCX revenue (has ranged widely as domestic Chinese chipmakers front-loaded capex ahead of anticipated further BIS restrictions, then air-pocketed). **This is a two-sided risk**: (a) further US export-control tightening could cut off high-growth China revenue directly; (b) the *absence* of new restrictions has been partly responsible for elevated recent-quarter China bookings that may not repeat (a pull-forward, not a sustainable run-rate) — either direction of policy surprise is a risk to the current growth trajectory the DCF/comps analysis extrapolates forward.

### 4. Competitive risk — ASML/AMAT overlap
While LRCX, AMAT, and ASML mostly occupy different process steps (deposition/etch vs. lithography vs. broad-portfolio), **advanced packaging and gate-all-around/backside-power delivery nodes are increasing overlap** at the tool-set level, and Chinese domestic equipment makers (e.g., AMEC, Naura) are a rising competitive threat specifically in mature/legacy nodes where China is investing most aggressively under self-sufficiency policy — a risk explicitly flagged in scout's own sourcing (Quiver Quantitative) as a contributing factor in the Sept 2026 sector selloff.

### 5. Valuation-reconciliation stress case
Applying a bottom-third self-history multiple (9.23x, the Method-1 B-V1 cutoff) to TTM revenue: FV/share = **$173.03**. Applying a genuinely bearish DCF (WACC 15%, Yr1 growth 5%, TGR 2.5%): FV/share = **$44.76**. **Bear FV = average of these two stress cases = $108.90/share** — implying **~65% downside** from the current $315.21 price under a coherent (not cherry-picked) bear scenario set.

### Bear Flip Triggers (would materially weaken the bear case / strengthen the bull case)
1. **Two consecutive quarters of sequential (not just YoY) revenue acceleration** in both logic/foundry AND memory segments, confirming broad-based (not narrow/pull-forward) demand.
2. **Independent confirmation that China revenue is NOT front-loaded/pull-forward** — e.g., management guidance explicitly reaffirming multi-year China demand visibility, not just a trailing print.
3. **No further US export-control tightening announced through 2H2026/1H2027** (status-quo or clarifying resolution, not escalation).
4. **EV/Revenue multiple compresses to bottom-third of 5Y self-history (≤9.23x) on stable/improving fundamentals** (i.e., price correction without a fundamental deterioration) — this is the single cleanest "buy the dip" signal the Bear case would accept.
5. **Customer concentration disclosure shows diversification** (no single customer >15% of revenue) reducing single-customer capex-pause risk.

**Bear Conviction input: Bear case is well-supported by real, sourced, non-speculative risks (cycle-timing, concentration, policy, competitive) and produces an FV range ($44.76-$173.03) that brackets and sits below both Emma's DCF and comps ranges — reinforcing rather than contradicting the base-case finding that price has run ahead of fundamentals.**
